## Abstract

Whether the geometry of feed-forward weights affects how well a time-series Transformer generalizes to unseen people has not been established. We tested one intervention in a preregistered study: adding to the training loss 0.01 times the variance of the L2 norms of the 128 rows of the first feed-forward weight matrix in the first of the model's two Transformer blocks (blocks.0.fc1.weight). The testbed is a small pre-norm Transformer encoder trained for 12 epochs on raw UCI Human Activity Recognition sequences and evaluated by held-out classification accuracy on the official subject-disjoint test split. The proposed condition was compared with an unregularized baseline and with a negative control that applies the same penalty to column norms, on 43 paired primary seeds and a pre-specified confirmatory replication on 43 disjoint seeds, using a paired bootstrap 95% confidence interval and a preregistered worthwhile margin of 0.0125 accuracy. The mean paired difference in accuracy (proposed minus baseline) was -0.00008 (95% CI [-0.00217, 0.00214]) in the primary cohort and 0.00094 (95% CI [-0.00180, 0.00387]) in the replication cohort. Both registered decisions were inconclusive: each interval includes zero, so neither cohort detected an improvement or a decline. Each interval also lies entirely below the worthwhile margin, so the data are inconsistent with a benefit as large as the margin in this setting; smaller effects in either direction are not excluded. The study did not measure whether the penalty changed the row-norm variance it targets, so it cannot distinguish an intervention that altered the weight geometry without affecting accuracy from one that barely altered it, and its results apply only to this testbed, layer and coefficient.

## 1. Introduction

Transformers have become prominent in time-series analysis, offering capabilities for modeling long-range dependencies that recurrent architectures often struggle to capture [15]. Despite their adoption, the efficacy of Transformer-based solutions for time-series tasks is scrutinized, with some work questioning their validity compared to simpler linear models in forecasting contexts [5]. A critical open problem lies in understanding which architectural components contribute to robustness under distribution shift, particularly when generalizing to unseen subjects. While attention mechanisms receive significant attention regarding consistency and stability [2], the role of the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) component in generalization remains less explored. Existing work examines attention consistency, input projection spectra, and general FFN capacity, but no provided study explicitly tests whether penalizing the variance of FFN neuron weight norms improves subject-disjoint generalization in time-series transformers.

This gap matters because clarifying whether FFN regularization is a viable path for robustness guides architecture design decisions for similar time-series Transformer applications. If weight geometry influences generalization, regularization strategies targeting FFN neurons could enhance model reliability without altering attention mechanisms. Conversely, if such interventions yield no benefit, resources may be better directed toward other inductive biases. This paper addresses this gap by measuring the effect of one operationalisation of FFN neuron capacity balancing, a row-norm variance penalty on blocks.0.fc1.weight, on held-out classification accuracy.

## 2. Related Work

Transformers have been widely adopted for time series analysis, though their efficacy is scrutinized. *Are Transformers Effective for Time Series Forecasting?* questions the validity of Transformer-based solutions for long-term forecasting, while *Transformers in Time Series: A Survey* documents challenges in time series analysis and *Out-of-Distribution Generalization in Time Series: A Survey* documents the growing interest and the challenges of distribution shifts in this domain [5, 15, 14]. Specific adaptations include *Knowledge Aggregation Transformer Network for Multivariate Time Series Classification*, which utilizes dual networks for feature extraction, and *Domain Adaptation for Time series Transformers using One-step fine-tuning*, which addresses prediction limitations through fine-tuning [7, 12]. These works establish the context of Transformer application but do not isolate FFN weight geometry as a variable for subject-disjoint generalization.

Research specifically targeting the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) component examines structural design and capacity control. *Investigating the Role of Feed-Forward Networks in Transformers Using Parallel Attention and Feed-Forward Net Design* compares parallel versus series attention and FFN designs to understand their key roles [11]. *FL-Tuning: Layer Tuning for Feed-Forward Network in Transformer* explores tuning FFN layers for downstream tasks, and *An Efficient Two-Stage Pipelined Compute-in-Memory Macro for Accelerating Transformer Feed-Forward Networks* focuses on hardware acceleration for these components [13, 10]. Foundational work such as *Regularization and complexity control in feed-forward networks* considers approaches like architecture selection and training with noise for complexity control [6]. Together, these papers establish that FFN capacity and design are adjustable levers, but they do not test weight norm variance regularization for generalization.

Regarding regularization and generalization strategies, several studies test weight geometry and consistency constraints. *Spectral Entropy Minimization of Input Projections for Temporal Feature Learning* tests whether enforcing a low-entropy singular value spectrum on input projection weights enhances held-out classification accuracy [4]. *Weight-Sharing Regularization* proposes a penalty on weight differences to encourage sharing [9]. In the context of subject-disjoint generalization, *Cross-Layer Query Consistency Loss Tests Whether Attention Stability Across Depth Improves Subject-Disjoint Generalization in Time-Series Transformers* tests enforcing consistency in attention query projections [2]. However, *Replication: Cross-Layer Query Consistency Loss Tests Whether Attention Stability Across Depth Improves Subject-Disjoint Generalization in Time-Series Transformers* discloses inconclusive decisions [1]. Additionally, *Entropy-Maximized Multiplicative Weight Noise for Subject-Disjoint Robustness* has been retracted by the journal because its executed intervention did not implement the preregistered one, although the retraction notice states that its numerical outputs remain computationally reproducible [3]. These findings position the current study against a backdrop of inconclusive or retracted results, motivating a rigorous test of FFN neuron capacity balancing.

A random split would leak subject identity into training, inflating accuracy and masking generalization failures. Thus, the subject-disjoint split was designed to test robustness to inter-subject variability.

## 6. Discussion

The principal finding is that FFN row-norm variance regularization did not yield robust accuracy gains in this regime. In the primary cohort, the effect was negligible and nonsignificant. In the replication cohort, the effect remained nonsignificant, with the confidence interval including zero. Both registered decisions are inconclusive, and both intervals lie below the worthwhile margin, so the data are inconsistent with an improvement as large as that margin. It does not establish that the effect is exactly zero, but it excludes gains of the target magnitude.

What this means for someone choosing between these methods is that there is no evidence to prefer the proposed condition over the baseline for subject-disjoint generalization in this setting. A mechanism that would produce the observed result is that weight norm uniformity does not influence generalization in finite-width time-series Transformers. An alternative mechanism is that the coefficient (0.01) was too small to induce a measurable change, or that the optimization noise overwhelmed the regularization signal. The pre-specified confirmatory replication cohort adds precision to the estimate but fails to resolve the inconclusive decision. The results agree with [2] and [4], which also found inconclusive results for attention consistency and spectral entropy minimization, respectively. Because the study did not measure the weight geometry, they do not test whether FFN weight geometry is a driver of generalization.

The findings stand against specific cited papers. [11] investigates FFN roles but does not test norm variance. [8] proposes capacity control but for memorization, not subject-disjoint generalization. [1], a replication of [2] that carries an erratum, also reports inconclusive decisions for attention consistency; the present study tested a different intervention, a row-norm variance penalty, and is likewise inconclusive. [3] is retracted and cannot be relied upon. The design does not establish out-of-distribution generalization beyond unseen subjects. Future work would settle the question by testing larger margins, different coefficients, or architectures where FFN capacity is more constrained.
